## Outcome
**DAU Composition:**
| Segment | % of DAU | Volume | Retention (Desktop–Mobile) |
|---------|----------|--------|---------------------------|
| New Accounts | 0.1% | 57K | 18% – 44% |
| Reactivated (gone 7-30 days) | 3% | 1.9M | 27% – 31% |
| Resurrected (gone >30 days) | 1% | 725K | 17% – 24% |
| Continuing (active past 7 days) | 95% | 55M | 70% – 80% |

**User Base Sizing (168M MAU):**
- IMAP Users: 63M (IMAP-only: 40M, IMAP+O&O: 23M)
- O&O Users: 120M (Desktop Web: 54M, Mobile: 94M)
- Mobile: Mobile Web 16M, Mobile App 82M (iOS 35M, Android 49M)
- "We don't have accurate tracking for IMAP, nor can we monetize those 3rd party client only users"

## Nuance
The 95% continuing user composition means that **DAU growth is fundamentally a retention and frequency game**, not an acquisition game. New accounts (0.1%) and resurrected users (1%) are rounding errors in the daily mix. This frames the entire growth strategy: the highest-leverage work is keeping existing users engaged (gamification [006-011], notifications [031-036]) and converting IMAP-Out users to O&O (IMAP-Out Rescue [038]).

The 40M IMAP-only users are the largest untapped segment — they use Yahoo Mail daily but through third-party clients, generating zero direct revenue. This is the strategic foundation for the entire IMAP-Out Rescue program.

Mobile retention is dramatically higher than desktop across all segments (e.g., new user: 44% mobile vs 18% desktop), reinforcing the strategic priority of shifting users from desktop/IMAP to mobile app [026].
