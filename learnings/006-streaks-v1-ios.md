## Hypothesis

A low-weight streak MVP — users receive credit for daily login (copy suggests reading/deleting, but just logging in counts) — will lift engagement, particularly among less-active user segments (AOT).

## Test Design

iOS users who open or delete an email are opted in. Three variations: Base Experience, Digital Reward Experience, Competitive Experience. Cohort + temporal analysis tracking DV7 before, during, and after streak. Eligible: users who open/delete email.

## Outcome

Massive DV7 lifts for less-active segments during streak: Active +17%, Occasional +37%, Tourist +62%. Significant post-streak residual: Active +3.67%, Occasional +16.42%, Tourist +45.21%. Fanatics and Loyalists barely moved (-1% to +2.85% during). AOT opt-out rate: 6-7%. FL opt-out rate: 10-12% (higher). Reward variant had slightly lower opt-out for AOT.

## Nuance

Streaks disproportionately impact users who aren't already habitual — Fanatics already have high DV7 so there's little room. The residual post-streak impact for O/T users is the key finding: the streak may be forming genuine habit changes, not just temporary behavior. FL having higher opt-out rates suggests they find it annoying/unnecessary since they already use the product daily. Social variant showed similar patterns to others. This was the foundational signal that justified Streaks v2 and the broader Challenges program.
